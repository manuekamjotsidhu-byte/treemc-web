Move a copy of your Aternos world first

Download the world from Aternos while you still have access to the original server. Record the edition, version, server software, add-ons, allowlist and settings. Save a separate copy of any files you can export and take notes on what each plugin stores. A world download primarily gives you the world. A plugin that keeps permissions, claims or balances in another file or database may need its own export path.

Start the new server on the same Minecraft version and compatible server software. Stop it before replacing the generated world. Import a copy and start it with a limited set of players. Visit a few familiar areas, check inventories and permissions, and travel to other dimensions you already use. Keep the old server available until someone has confirmed that the copy behaves as expected.

Agree on a handover time for a shared community. After that time, keep the old world closed for building while you check the new one. Two active copies make it difficult to decide which player changes to keep. Share the new address only after the test, and keep the original export somewhere outside the new host.

Start with the reason you want to move

Perhaps your friends cannot join when they want, the required software is unavailable or you need more control over files. Record the problem precisely and read your current provider's terms. Free services differ, so do not assume that every one has the same startup rules, queues or resource limits.

If the existing service works for a casual world, a subscription is not automatically necessary. If it stops the group from playing, use the missing capability as a requirement for the next plan.

Check what the paid product actually includes

Compare access, supported software, storage, allocation, backups and service policies. A paid plan does not inherently include migration assistance, unlimited resources or management of your plugins. Ask how those responsibilities are split and what the stated availability commitment covers.

Use the Minecraft hosting buying guide to form a shortlist. Keep any unanswered question visible instead of interpreting a marketing word as a technical specification.

Put a cost on the ordinary month

Agree on a monthly limit that the group can maintain. Check the regular renewal amount and the total at checkout, not only an introductory price. A tiny entry plan can be useful for a suitable workload but should not be stretched to fit a modpack with different requirements.

TreeMC publishes several Minecraft plan tiers. Compare the product you need against your current service; this article is written by TreeMC and is not an independent ranking of free providers. The budget guide includes a contribution example.

Prove that your world can move

Before cancelling anything, check the export process and preserve an independent copy of the world and configuration. Record the server version, plugins and modpack release. Credentials and player information should be handled privately during the move.

Import into a test setup and verify that the world, inventories and permissions behave as expected. Our moving-host guide and SFTP guide cover the preparation. Keep the old copy until the new server has been checked.

Judge the result against the original problem

After moving, ask whether the specific restriction or interruption has improved. Repeat an ordinary play session, check access and restore a backup. A new monthly payment is not proof that everything is better.

If the issue remains, collect logs and ask for help with the evidence. Review cancellation and refund terms before purchase, since a disappointing result does not automatically create an entitlement to a refund. Choose a service because its terms and capabilities fit the group.
