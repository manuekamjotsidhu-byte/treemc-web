Ping, server location and DDoS protection

By TreeMC · Updated 17 November 2026

How to read ping, latency and packet loss

  • Ping (latency) is how long data takes to reach the server and come back, in milliseconds. Under 50 ms feels instant in games, 50 to 100 ms is fine for most games, above 150 ms feels laggy.
  • Jitter is how much ping jumps around. A steady 60 ms plays better than ping that swings between 20 and 120 ms.
  • Packet loss is data that never arrives. Even 1 to 2% causes rubber-banding and stutter. It should be 0%.

Test it yourself

  • Windows: ping -n 50 your.server.ip. Look at the average time and "Lost" at the end.
  • Mac or Linux: ping -c 50 your.server.ip.
  • Find where it goes wrong: tracert your.server.ip on Windows or mtr your.server.ip on Linux shows each hop. Loss that starts at the first hop is your Wi-Fi or router; loss in the middle is your internet provider's route.

Some servers block ping. In that case use the in-game ping or the game's server browser.

If your players are spread out, choose the place in the middle of them, not the one closest to you. Wi-Fi adds ping and loss; a cable to the router helps more than any server change.

What is DDoS protection?

A DDoS (distributed denial of service) attack floods your server with junk traffic from many machines so real players cannot connect. Game servers get hit often, sometimes by a player who just got banned.

DDoS protection filters traffic at the network edge, before it reaches your server. Attack traffic is dropped and normal players keep playing, usually without noticing anything.

  • It does protect against: floods that try to fill your connection or overload the network, which are the vast majority of attacks.
  • It does not protect against: weak passwords, leaked tokens, malicious plugins or someone who has your admin login. Keep those safe yourself.
  • Do not share your home IP. On a hosted server, players only ever see the server's protected address.
